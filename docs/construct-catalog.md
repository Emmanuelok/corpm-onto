## Corrupt act

An improper act, scheme, or behaviour through which an actor seeks undue private or political benefit in a project context.

- **Abuse of authority**: Improper use of official or organisational authority to distort project outcomes.
- **Abuse of discretion**: Improper exploitation of discretionary authority for undue benefit.
- **Asset misappropriation**: Improper diversion, theft, or misuse of project funds, materials, or other resources.
- **Bribery**: Offering, giving, receiving, or soliciting an undue advantage to influence a project-related decision or action.
- **Collusion**: Coordination among actors to distort competition, oversight, pricing, allocation, or certification.
- **Confidential information leakage**: Improper disclosure of confidential project or procurement information to advantage a party.
- **Conflict of interest**: A corrupt or unmanaged conflict between public, project, fiduciary, or professional duty and private interest.
- **Embezzlement**: Misappropriation of funds or assets entrusted to an actor in a project setting.
- **Extortion**: Obtaining benefit through coercion, threat, or abuse of power.
- **Favoritism**: Preferential treatment of a favoured actor without fair, transparent, or merit-based justification.
- **Fraud**: Intentional deception or misrepresentation for improper project benefit.
- **Political interference**: Improper political intervention in technical, procurement, funding, or oversight decisions.
- **Procurement manipulation**: Manipulation of procurement planning, solicitation, evaluation, or award to produce improper benefit.
- **Regulatory capture**: A situation in which regulatory processes are dominated by the interests they are meant to oversee.

## Cause

A causal condition that helps explain why corruption emerges or persists in a project environment.

- **Budget pressure**: Budget strain or fiscal pressure that distorts project decisions or weakens assurance.
- **Cultural normalization**: A cultural context in which corrupt practices become normalised, tolerated, or rationalised.
- **Lack of transparency**: Insufficient public, institutional, or stakeholder visibility over project decisions, data, or transactions.
- **Political patronage**: Political influence or networks that distort project decisions, appointments, or awards for favour rather than merit.
- **Time pressure**: Schedule urgency that incentivises shortcuts, weak review, or emergency decision-making.
- **Weak accountability**: Limited ability to hold actors answerable for project decisions, misconduct, or poor performance.
- **Weak governance**: Weak governance arrangements that fail to define clear accountability, controls, or oversight.
- **Weak sanctions**: Weak or inconsistently applied sanctions that reduce deterrence.

- **Complex supply chain**: Supply chain complexity that obscures accountability, pricing, beneficial ownership, or delivery quality.
- **Discretionary decision making**: Decision-making arrangements with high discretion and weak review, documentation, or challenge.
- **Emergency procurement**: Urgent procurement conditions that compress review, competition, or documentation.
- **Fragmented project delivery**: Fragmented delivery arrangements that diffuse accountability across many interfaces.
- **Inadequate competition**: Low or distorted competition that increases risk of collusion, capture, or inflated pricing.
- **Inadequate digital traceability**: Insufficient digital records, interoperability, or transaction traceability for reliable anti-corruption analytics.
- **Information asymmetry**: Uneven access to information across actors, creating opportunities for manipulation or concealment.
- **Low detection probability**: A condition in which corrupt acts are unlikely to be observed, flagged, or investigated.
- **Monopoly power**: Market or institutional concentration that limits competition and increases capture risk.
- **Opaque procurement**: Procurement conditions with weak disclosure, traceability, auditability, or market visibility.
- **Poor contract management**: Weak contract administration, monitoring, or documentation that creates exploitable gaps.
- **Poor record keeping**: Insufficient record quality, completeness, or retention that weakens traceability and oversight.
- **Poor stakeholder oversight**: Weak involvement of funders, communities, users, or oversight actors in project scrutiny.
- **Weak audit capacity**: Insufficient audit capability, access, independence, tools, or resources.

## Red flag

- **Abnormal payment pattern**: A red flag in which payment timing, amount, or routing behaves unexpectedly.

- **Delayed inspection reports**: A red flag in which inspection or supervision reports are delayed beyond credible operational explanations.
- **Excessive use of emergency procurement**: A red flag in which emergency procurement procedures are used repeatedly or beyond credible emergency need.
- **Frequent variation orders**: A red flag in which variations or change orders occur unusually frequently.
- **Front-loaded payments**: A red flag in which unusually large payments occur early relative to certified progress.
- **Identical bid documents**: A red flag in which separate bids contain highly similar formatting, wording, or errors.
- **Inflated unit prices**: A red flag in which unit prices appear materially above defensible market benchmarks.
- **Large cost increase after award**: A red flag in which the contract price increases materially after award without a convincing justification.
- **Low competition**: A red flag indicating a pattern of weak or unusually constrained competition.
- **Missing conflict of interest declarations**: A red flag in which required declarations are absent, incomplete, or unverifiable.
- **Poor documentation**: A red flag in which expected records are missing, inconsistent, or incomplete.
- **Quality defects despite certification**: A red flag in which visible quality defects appear despite prior certification or approval.
- **Repeated change orders**: A red flag in which change orders recur in a way that suggests manipulation rather than legitimate adaptation.
- **Repeated contract awards to same firm**: A red flag in which one firm repeatedly wins comparable contracts without a clear legitimate explanation.
- **Single bidder**: A red flag in which only one bid is received in a context where competition would normally be expected.
- **Split contracts**: A red flag in which work is fragmented into smaller contracts to avoid thresholds or scrutiny.
- **Tailored specifications**: A red flag in which specifications appear tailored to favour a particular bidder, product, or consultant.
- **Unexplained scope changes**: A red flag in which project scope changes are weakly justified, poorly documented, or inconsistent with need.
- **Unusual subcontracting pattern**: A red flag in which subcontracting relationships or chains appear inconsistent with market or delivery logic.
- **Unusually short tender period**: A red flag in which the tender period is unusually short relative to project complexity or market practice.
- **Vague technical specifications**: A red flag in which specifications are too vague to support fair, testable, or comparable competition.
